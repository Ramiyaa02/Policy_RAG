1. Dataset Collection & Corpus Definition 
1.1 Purpose 
The purpose of dataset collection is to establish a reliable, domain-specific, traceable, and 
controlled knowledge corpus for the Insurance RAG AI Assistant. 
The system is designed to answer insurance-related questions using evidence retrieved from 
the selected policy documents rather than relying exclusively on the pretrained knowledge of 
the underlying Large Language Model (LLM). 
The dataset therefore forms the authoritative knowledge boundary of the initial system. 
The collection process is designed to ensure that: 
 
the selected documents are relevant to the intended insurance use case;  
 
each document can be associated with a specific insurer and product;  
 
the source of policy information can be traced;  
 
different products are represented independently;  
 
unnecessary duplication is avoided;  
 
the corpus remains extensible for future products and policy versions.  
 
1.2 Business Problem Addressed by the 
Dataset 
Insurance products contain information distributed across lengthy and highly detailed policy 
documents. 
Relevant information may include: 
 
coverage and benefits;  
 
eligibility;  
 
definitions;  
 
waiting periods;  
 
exclusions;  
 
financial limits and sub-limits;  
 
optional covers;  
 
deductibles and co-payments;  
 
claim conditions;  
 
renewal conditions;  
 
portability and migration rules;  
 
product-specific clauses and exceptions.  

--- Page 2 ---
A general-purpose LLM may understand the general concept of health insurance but cannot 
be assumed to know the exact contractual conditions of a particular insurance product. 
For example, the Star Comprehensive documentation specifies a 36-month waiting period 
for pre-existing diseases, while separately providing an optional mechanism to reduce the 
waiting period to 12 months.  
Therefore, the system requires access to the actual product-specific source documents. 
 
1.3 Dataset Objective 
The dataset is designed to support four primary capabilities: 
1. Product-specific Question Answering 
Example: 
"What is the waiting period for pre-existing diseases under Star Comprehensive?" 
2. Evidence-based Question Answering 
Example: 
"What expenses are covered before hospitalization?" 
3. Cross-product Comparison 
Example: 
"Compare the pre-existing disease waiting periods across the available policies." 
4. Product-aware Retrieval 
The system must distinguish between information belonging to different insurers and 
products. 
For example: 
User Query 
     ↓ 
Identify relevant product(s) 
     ↓ 
Retrieve evidence 
     ↓ 
Verify product/source 
     ↓ 
Generate answer

1.6 Selection Criteria 
A document is considered suitable for inclusion when it satisfies the following criteria.

1.6.1 Domain Relevance 
The document must belong to the insurance domain targeted by the project. 
1.6.2 Product Relevance 
The document must correspond to one of the explicitly selected insurance products. 
1.6.3 Source Authority 
Preference is given to documents originating from the respective insurer or an authoritative 
public source. 
1.6.4 Product Specificity 
The document must contain product-specific information rather than only generic insurance 
education. 
1.6.5 Information Depth 
The document should contain sufficient detail to support meaningful question answering and 
comparison. 
1.6.6 Traceability 
The source must allow retrieved information to be associated with the originating document 
and product. 
1.6.7 Version Identification 
Where available, identifiers such as UIN, version, policy number reference, or document date 
are retained. 
For example, the Star Comprehensive documents identify the product as Star 
Comprehensive Insurance Policy with UIN SHAHLIP26044V092526.

1.8 Why Synthetic Data Was Not Selected 
as the Primary Corpus 
Synthetic policy documents were considered unsuitable as the primary dataset. 
Synthetic documents can be useful for controlled unit testing, but they have a major 
limitation: 
Their structure and content are known and controlled by the developer. 
For example, a synthetic policy could intentionally be created as:

 ├── Waiting Period 
 ├── Exclusions 
 ├── Claims 
 └── Renewal 
This makes document processing and retrieval significantly easier.

1.9 Why Five Products Were Selected 
The project does not aim to build a single-document question-answering system. 
It is intended to support multi-product insurance information retrieval and comparison. 
A single policy would allow us to test: 
"What does this policy say?" 
But it would not adequately test: 
"How does this policy differ from another policy?" 
Therefore, multiple products are required. 
Five products provide an initial balance between: 
 
corpus diversity;

 
manageable development scope;  
 
meaningful cross-product comparison;  
 
retrieval evaluation;  
 
implementation complexity.  
The corpus can later be expanded without redesigning the overall dataset strategy. 
 
1.10 Why Products From Different Insurers 
Were Selected 
The selected products come from different insurance providers. 
This is intentional. 
The project assumes that different insurers may define similar insurance concepts differently. 
For example, two policies may both discuss: 
"Pre-existing disease" 
but may differ in: 
 
waiting period;  
 
conditions;  
 
exceptions;  
 
applicability;  
 
optional modifications.  
Therefore, the system must not treat insurance terminology as universally interchangeable. 
Instead, the source product must remain identifiable throughout the system. 
The dataset therefore introduces a deliberate cross-insurer information variation. 
 
1.11 Why We Selected One Primary Policy 
Document Per Product 
Insurance providers commonly publish several documents for a product: 
 
Policy wording;  
 
Prospectus;  
 
Customer Information Sheet;

 
Brochure;  
 
Claim forms;  
 
Product guides.  
For the initial corpus, the project uses the detailed policy wording / policy terms and 
conditions as the primary source. 
Reason 
The detailed policy document contains the product-specific conditions needed for reliable 
policy-level question answering. 
The Star Comprehensive Customer Information Sheet explicitly identifies itself as a key-
information document and advises the policyholder to refer to the policy document.  
Therefore, the initial corpus follows: 
One product → one primary authoritative policy document. 
Supporting documents are not included merely because they are available. 
They will only be added when a specific system requirement justifies their inclusion. 
 
1.12 Why We Do Not Collect Every 
Available Document 
Collecting every document published by an insurer can introduce: 
 
duplicate information;  
 
conflicting summaries;  
 
multiple representations of the same clause;  
 
unnecessary retrieval candidates;  
 
increased corpus maintenance requirements.  
For example, the same benefit may appear in both a prospectus and a customer information 
sheet. 
Rather than increasing corpus size without a defined purpose, the initial implementation 
establishes a controlled knowledge boundary. 
This improves: 
 
source traceability;  
 
retrieval interpretation;  
 
corpus maintenance;  
 
evaluation consistency.

1.13 Source-of-Truth Strategy 
The original policy documents are retained as the source-of-truth layer. 
They are not manually rewritten or replaced with generated summaries. 
The conceptual relationship is: 
Official/Public Policy Document 
            │ 
            ▼ 
       Corpus Source 
            │ 
            ▼ 
     Processed Dataset 
            │ 
            ▼ 
      Retrieval System 
The processed dataset must remain traceable to the original document. 
This is particularly important for conditional insurance clauses. 
For example, the Star Comprehensive policy contains both a general 36-month PED waiting 
period and an optional reduction to 12 months.  
A manually simplified dataset could accidentally retain only one of these facts and produce a 
misleading answer. 
Therefore, the original source is always retained for verification.

The dataset has several known limitations.

1.22 Dataset Collection Completion 
Definition 
Dataset Collection is considered complete when: 
1. The target products have been explicitly defined.  
2. The five selected products have been collected.  
3. Each product has an identified primary policy document.  
4. Original source documents have been preserved.  
5. Product and insurer identity has been recorded.  
6. Available UIN/version information has been recorded.  
7. Source and collection information has been recorded.  
8. Duplicate/supporting documents have been evaluated.  
9. Dataset assumptions and limitations have been documented.  
10. Corpus governance and versioning rules have been established.  
 
1.23 Final Corpus Design Decision 
The final decision for Phase 1 is: 
The Insurance RAG system will initially use a controlled corpus of five publicly 
available, product-specific insurance policy documents sourced across five different 
insurers. One primary policy document is maintained for each selected product, while 
the original documents are preserved as the source of truth. Synthetic data is excluded 
from the primary knowledge corpus because it does not adequately represent real-
world policy variability. The corpus is deliberately limited in size for controlled 
development and evaluation, while its design allows future expansion to additional 
insurers, products, and policy versions.

16. What We Will NOT Do During 
Extraction 
This distinction should be very clear to the team. 
We will NOT: 
❌ summarize the PDF 
❌ ask an LLM to rewrite the policy 
❌ merge all PDFs into one text 
❌ split every page into a chunk 
❌ split every 500 tokens blindly 
❌ discard page information 
❌ flatten tables without structure 
❌ assume all PDFs have the same layout 
We WILL: 
✅ preserve the original PDFs 
✅ extract page-level content 
✅ preserve layout information 
✅ identify document elements 
✅ preserve tables 
✅ preserve source locations 
✅ preserve document identity 
✅ create an intermediate structured representation

Extraction

Semantic chunking.

1. Introduction 
1.1 Purpose 
This document specifies the requirements for an Insurance Policy Intelligence & Agentic 
RAG Assistant, an AI-powered decision-support system designed to help users understand, 
retrieve, compare, and reason over information contained in insurance policy documents. 
Insurance products are typically described through lengthy and heterogeneous documents 
such as policy wordings, brochures, terms and conditions, eligibility criteria, exclusions, 
benefits, waiting-period clauses, claim procedures, and product descriptions. 
Finding relevant information manually across these documents can be time-consuming and 
may result in inconsistent interpretation. 
The proposed system will use Large Language Models (LLMs), Retrieval-Augmented 
Generation (RAG), semantic retrieval, agentic workflows, and evidence-grounding 
mechanisms to provide answers that remain connected to the available insurance 
documentation. 
The system is intended to function as an information and decision-support assistant, not as 
an autonomous insurance underwriting, legal, medical, or financial decision-maker. 
 
1.2 Problem Statement 
A conventional document search system requires users to manually identify relevant 
documents and sections before interpreting the information. 

--- Page 2 ---
A conventional LLM-based chatbot introduces another problem: the model may generate 
information that is plausible but unsupported by the underlying insurance documents. 
The system therefore needs to solve two related problems: 
1. Information retrieval: Identify the relevant evidence from a large collection of 
insurance documents. 
2. Evidence-grounded reasoning: Generate useful responses while preventing 
unsupported claims from being presented as facts. 
The system should additionally support complex queries requiring information from multiple 
sections or products. 
Example: 
"Compare these three health insurance policies for hospitalization coverage, waiting period, 
exclusions, and eligibility for a 45-year-old applicant." 
This cannot always be answered reliably through a single retrieval-generation step. 
 
2. Project Objectives 
The primary objectives are: 
O1 — Document Intelligence 
Convert heterogeneous insurance documents into a searchable, structured knowledge source. 
O2 — Semantic Retrieval 
Retrieve relevant policy information based on the meaning of the user's query rather than 
only exact keyword matches. 
O3 — Contextual Question Answering 
Generate answers using retrieved insurance evidence as the primary knowledge source. 
O4 — Agentic Query Processing 
Decompose complex insurance queries into smaller information-gathering and reasoning 
tasks. 
O5 — Product Comparison 
Compare multiple insurance products according to user-specified criteria. 

--- Page 3 ---
O6 — Evidence Grounding 
Associate generated claims with supporting source passages. 
O7 — Hallucination Control 
Prevent unsupported information from being presented as policy fact. 
O8 — Evidence-Aware Abstention 
When sufficient evidence is unavailable, the system should explicitly communicate the 
limitation and request clarification or additional information rather than fabricate an answer. 
O9 — Measurable Evaluation 
Evaluate the system against a simpler RAG baseline using objective technical metrics.

26. Key Design Principle 
The central design principle of the system is: 
The assistant should optimize for evidence-supported usefulness rather than confident 
generation. 
A successful response is therefore not necessarily the longest or most confident response. 
A successful response is one that: 
1. Understands the user's intent. 
2. Retrieves relevant evidence. 
3. Reasons over that evidence appropriately. 
4. Distinguishes facts from conclusions. 
5. Provides traceable sources. 

--- Page 29 ---
6. Identifies insufficient evidence. 
7. Avoids unsupported claims. 
8. Requests clarification when necessary. 
This principle will guide both system implementation and evaluation.
